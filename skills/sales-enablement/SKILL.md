# Sales Enablement

Enable a seller to advance a real buyer decision. Start with the sales motion, segment, deal stage, product maturity, and observed friction—not a request for generic collateral.

## Diagnose the enablement gap

Clarify the audience of the asset (seller, partner, manager), the customer role, the decision to advance, and the evidence of the problem. Review representative call notes, win/loss data, deal stages, existing assets, and product constraints when available. Separate a messaging issue from a product, pricing, targeting, process, or training issue.

Design for the moments that matter: prospecting, discovery, qualification, evaluation, security/procurement, business case, negotiation, onboarding handoff, or expansion. Give sellers questions that uncover fit, approved claims and proof, common objections, and a clear next step. Never recommend misrepresentation, pressure tactics, or unapproved competitive statements.

## Make assets usable

Keep each asset purpose-specific and easy to find. Pair external-facing material with internal guidance on when to use it, for whom, what to customize, required approvals, and how success is measured. Test with sellers in realistic scenarios and collect feedback from customer conversations.

## Deliverable

Return an enablement plan with priority gaps, asset map, audience/stage coverage, draft outline or copy where requested, training/practice plan, owner, adoption measures, and business outcomes to monitor. Do not modify a CRM, send material, certify sellers, or distribute externally without approval.
